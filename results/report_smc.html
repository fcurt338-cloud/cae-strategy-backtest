The mechanic, quantified

Every rule below is price-only (BTC is used as a reference series for SMT, not an indicator) plus one optional volume filter on the displacement candle — nothing lagging, no oscillators.

1 · Key levelCluster fractal swing lows into equal-lows liquidity pools (within 0.6% of each other)
→
2 · SweepPrice wicks below the pool, closes back above it within 3 bars
→
3 · SMT divergenceBTC does not make its own corresponding new low in the same window
→
4 · FVG formsA bullish 3-candle imbalance appears within the next 20 bars
→
5 · EntryLimit fill at the FVG midpoint on retracement, within a 40-bar window

Stop sits below the sweep low (ATR-buffered); target is the nearest confirmed opposing swing high that clears a 1.5R minimum (setups below that bar are skipped, not force-fit — an early sanity check caught several setups where the nearest target was closer than the stop, which would have been a guaranteed-negative-expectancy trade by construction). Once the target partial-fills, the remainder trails on the most recent confirmed swing low — structure, not an ATR multiple.

Four in five trades end the same way: price fills the FVG, then keeps going — closing back below the gap's low bound instead of holding it as support. The entry thesis is "this imbalance represents real, unfinished demand that will hold on a retest." On this data, it usually doesn't. Sweep-and-reclaim plus BTC non-confirmation identifies something happening, but not reliably a genuine reversal — on thin low-cap books a sweep can just as easily be the first leg of continued distribution as it can be a shakeout.

Requiring the displacement candle to clear 1.5× its 20-bar average volume filters out weak imbalances and genuinely helps — win rate up 19–42% in relative terms, profit factor up 25–37%. fvg_invalidated is still ~82% of exits either way. This is a real, valid improvement; it just isn't close to enough on its own.

0 of 50 symbols were net profitable on either timeframe — systemic, same as CAE, not a few outliers.

Reading this against the CAE result

This is the more useful finding than either backtest alone. Two structurally different, independently-designed price-action systems were built on this same universe:

  • CAE buys strength — a confirmed volume/range breakout out of a coil. It failed because ~46% of breakouts reversed almost immediately (spike-and-fade).
  • Liquidity Sweep + FVG buys weakness — a retracement into a demand zone after a stop-hunt. It failed because ~81% of those demand zones got run through instead of holding.

These are close to opposite entry philosophies, tested with different exit logic, different position-management, different indicators entirely — and they land in the same place: near-total loss, near-zero profit factor, zero profitable symbols out of fifty, both timeframes. That convergence is the signal. It's much more consistent with this specific universe being extremely difficult to trade long-only on pure price action — low-float order books thin enough that both breakouts and stop-hunts get faked constantly, in both directions — than with either rule set being individually miscalibrated. A parameter sweep already showed CAE has no rescue configuration; the volume filter here shows the same shape of result for this strategy.

Where I'd actually spend the next round

  1. Tighten what counts as a valid SMT divergence. The default threshold accepts BTC failing to make a new low by any margin at all, however small — which happens often by chance in a broader downtrend. Requiring BTC to hold meaningfully above its prior low (not just marginally) should cut a lot of low-conviction signals.
  2. Require confirmation before the fill, not just at the fill. Right now entry is a pure limit order at the FVG midpoint — the first touch, no reaction required. Waiting for a bar to close back above the FVG's low after tagging it (trading a touch-and-hold instead of a touch alone) directly targets the 81% invalidation rate, at the cost of worse average entry price.
  3. Test a peer/basket reference instead of BTC. BTC's volatility character is very different from a low-float altcoin's; a sector-relative or equal-weighted low-cap basket might be a tighter, more meaningful "smart money" comparison than the market benchmark.
  4. Cross this with the CAE finding rather than iterating further blind. Given two independent approaches both failed the same way, the next highest-value step may be diagnosing the underlying market microstructure itself (wash-trading prevalence, book depth, delisting/rug rates in this cap band) before building a third price-action variant on the same universe.
