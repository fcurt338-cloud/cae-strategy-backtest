Result Both timeframes wipe out the account. This holds under every single-parameter variation tested (32 sweep configurations), under an added exit-timing refinement, and it is not a sizing or accounting bug — it traces to the entry logic itself: confirmed breakouts fail and close back below the trigger level almost immediately, 46–47% of the time. Full diagnosis below.

What was built

Five modules, no third-party frameworks beyond pandas/numpy: a universe builder (Binance exchangeInfo + CoinGecko market caps), a resumable OHLCV downloader, vectorized CAE signal generation, a portfolio-level event-driven backtester (shared equity, max 4 concurrent positions, realistic fills), and a parameter sweep. Every rule from the spec is implemented literally — coil detection, five-condition breakout entry, the full exit stack (structural/ATR stop, scaled TP1, ATR/swing-low trailing, 48h time stop, coil-reentry invalidation, volume-fade exit), 0.75% risk sizing, and 0.1% fee + 1.5% slippage per side.

Universe: every live Binance USDT spot pair (475) cross-referenced against CoinGecko market cap, kept if $1M–$100M, then narrowed to the top 50 by 24h Binance quote volume so the full multi-year 15m+1h history could actually be fetched and simulated in this session — not literally every qualifying low-cap pair (there were 302 that cleared the market-cap screen). Each symbol also passes the spec's own filters live inside the backtest: 14-day listing-age exclusion and a rolling 7-day $100k average-volume gate that masks entries (not the underlying price series, which would corrupt the rolling coil/ATR math) during illiquid stretches.

Two honest caveats: (1) market cap is CoinGecko's current snapshot applied across the whole 2023–2026 window, not point-in-time history — a coin worth $60M today may have been a $400M mid-cap in 2024. (2) Only pairs still live on Binance today could be included, so tokens that were delisted or went to zero along the way are absent — a survivorship bias that should make these results look better than live trading, not worse. The strategy still failed comprehensively.

Both curves plunge to near-zero within the first months of 2023 and stay flat — there is no recovery, no cyclicality, no regime where the strategy turns around. 2023 alone accounts for essentially the entire loss (−$9,719 of the $10,000 starting balance on 15m; −$8,839 on 1h); 2024–2026 trades are fought over the last few dollars.

Root cause: this is a false-breakout problem, not a bug

Before trusting these numbers I found and fixed one real implementation bug: on a tight coil the structural/ATR stop can sit only ~1–2% from entry, and naive risk_amount / stop_distance sizing was pushing 50%+ of account equity into single trades. I capped position notional to an equal-weight slice of equity (equity ÷ max concurrent positions) — standard for a spot account with no leverage. After that fix, losses became sane in dollar terms, but the strategy was still catastrophic — which is when I confirmed this is a signal-quality problem, not a plumbing problem.

Almost half of every trade, on both timeframes, ends in coil_reentry: price closes back below the exact breakout level within one to a few bars of entry. The entry rules already demand a lot — close above the coil high, volume ≥3× the coil average, bar range ≥1.5× coil ATR, a bullish candle closing in its upper half — and it still doesn't screen out spike-and-fade. On thin low-cap books, a single large market order (or a wash trade) can produce exactly this signature: a volume and range spike with no genuine follow-through demand behind it. That is the textbook microstructure risk this strategy is trying to trade around, and in this data it dominates instead.

Average hold time is 1.1 hours on 15m and 4.3 hours on 1h — nowhere near long enough for the +40% TP1 target to ever be reached. Not one of the 6,537 15m trades or 1,851 1h trades exited via tp1. Every "win" in the results is a trade that happened to be closed above entry by volume_fade or coil_reentry, not a trade that hit its profit target.

I tested whether the exits were the problem — they aren't

Adding a grace period (no volume-fade or time-stop exits for the first 8 bars on 15m / 3 bars on 1h) changed almost nothing: 15m win rate moved from 3.85% to 4.29%, still −100% return; 1h was numerically identical, because no fade/time exits were even firing inside that window. coil_reentry isn't gated by a grace period by design (it's meant to invalidate the setup fast) and it alone accounts for the bulk of losses — so the exit stack isn't the leak. The failure is upstream, in which breakouts the entry rules accept.

"Best parameter set"

Answered as asked, but flagged for what it actually is: the least-catastrophic configuration found is 1-hour timeframe, coil_lookback=12 (profit factor 0.233, win rate 8.95%, max drawdown still −100% over the full 3.6-year run). It is the top of a sweep where every result lost the account. There is no parameter set in the tested ranges — 32 configurations across 8 parameters, plus a two-stage refinement — that turns this into a viable strategy on this data. Reporting it as "best" without that context would be misleading.

Suggested refinements

In order of how directly each targets the diagnosed cause — all pure price/volume, nothing lagging or oscillator-based:

  1. Require a held breakout, not a single-bar spike. I attempted a "2 consecutive closes above the coil high" filter and it produced zero trades on both timeframes — not a market finding, an implementation artifact: the coil-high level is recomputed per bar from a trailing window that already includes the prior bar, so requiring the prior bar's close to also clear that same level is a mathematical impossibility, not a real constraint. The right version freezes the coil boundary at the moment the coil forms (before any breakout attempt) and then requires N consecutive closes above that fixed level. Worth implementing properly — it directly targets the 46% coil-reentry failure mode — but it needs a small restructure of the signal window and wasn't validated here.
  2. Add a same-bar dollar-volume floor on the trigger candle itself, separate from the 7-day universe filter — e.g., require the breakout bar's own quote volume above some absolute USD minimum. A 3× multiplier over a coil average is easy to trip with a single large order on a book with $100k/day of trailing volume; an absolute floor makes that order need to be genuinely large in dollar terms, not just large relative to a quiet coil.
  3. Require the breakout to clear the coil high by a minimum buffer (e.g. 1–2%), not just close above it by any amount. Tiny breakouts are the ones most likely to be noise, and they also produce the thinnest stops that (before the fix applied here) blow up position sizing.
  4. Re-test the optional upper-range-position filter ("price in the upper 40% of its 7d/14d range") — specified as optional and not included in the sweep here. Restricting entries to coils that formed after an established uptrend, rather than a coil found anywhere in a chart, could filter out the many downtrend-context breakouts that are more likely bull traps.
  5. Widen the volume-fade condition's timeframe or drop it in favor of relying on the trailing stop alone post-TP1 — it wasn't the primary leak here, but at 39% of exits it's still cutting real winners short given TP1 was never once reached.

None of these guarantee a profitable system — a 46% immediate-failure rate on the core breakout signal is a large gap to close, and low-cap books may simply generate more false breakouts than genuine ones regardless of confirmation logic. They're the highest-leverage next experiments, in order.
